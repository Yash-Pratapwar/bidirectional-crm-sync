# Phase 1: assumptions and feasibility

Prepared 24 November 2026 from `Round1-Tequity.html` and `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`. This is a provisional contract for later design phases, not verification of Salesforce features. No real Salesforce behavior has been tested or researched. The brief establishes HTTPS webhooks and create/update APIs; the additional capabilities below remain questions for the client/integration owner. Phase 2 will define conflict comparisons and worked outcomes; subsequent phases will define transactions, flows, and full recovery accounting.

## Scope and known requirements

Our side synchronizes customers (name, status, owner) and contacts (full name, phone, email, city, owner, customer reference). Production has five million combined records and PostgreSQL. Both systems create and update. Distinct edits, including losing and intermediate edits, must retain values, actor, and time. Deletes, UI, login screens, second region, real Salesforce load testing, and production hardening are excluded. The introductory billing-city example does not expand the explicit field list: city belongs to contacts pending clarification (Q18).

Choose one application with HTTP and worker roles communicating through durable database state; detailed boundaries belong to Phase 3. Any optional demo uses TypeScript on Node.js 20+, fake Salesforce, and local durable persistence requiring no database server, Redis, or Docker. Local persistence demonstrates durability concepts; it does not establish production PostgreSQL throughput or crash guarantees.

## Workload and conditional latency envelope

These are planning assumptions, not measured throughput or an unconditional SLA. An edit is one committed create or field patch on one record; a multi-field patch counts once. Rates exclude generated integration echoes, which must be budgeted separately.

| Traffic | Sustained peak, including six-hour outage | Quiet hour | Burst allowance |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Portal-origin edits | 20 edits/s | 1 edit/s | 40 edits/s for at most 5 s, then return to 20/s |
| Salesforce-origin edits | 20 edits/s | 1 edit/s | 40 edits/s for at most 5 s, then return to 20/s |

Bursts may coincide. Each direction therefore adds at most 100 edits above baseline per burst. Assume no repeated burst until its excess queue drains, and no single hot record exceeds 1 edit/s sustained or 5 edits in a burst. Record count alone does not determine throughput.

Provisional amplification: one portal edit needs one outbound field-patch/create request; ordinary inbound edits need no read or write back. Budget up to 10 additional outbound requests/s in aggregate for retries, conflict reads/repairs, dependency lookups, and recovery pages. This is a bounded workload assumption, not a demonstrated ceiling: races can require a read and another conditional write. No coalescing is assumed. With peak portal writes, baseline outbound demand is at most 30/s; quiet demand is at most 11/s under that conservative overhead allowance. Integration echoes may add approximately 20 inbound events/s at peak (1/s quiet); assume total inbound delivery processing capacity of at least 200 events/s, including bounded duplicates, echoes, and replay. Confirm with production sizing later.

Operate at 90 outbound requests/s under the absolute 100/s cap. Every outbound attempt, retry, read, recovery request, and repair shares this budget, even though the brief explicitly names the record API cap. Inbound webhook deliveries consume processing capacity but no outbound request token. Assume the upstream permits this operating rate without a tighter daily quota or throttling. If throttled, retain work and reduce requests; the latency target may become infeasible.

The normal-operation 30-second worst case needs explicit bounds: source commit to webhook delivery <=5 s, network/API completion <=5 s, portal processing/commit <=2 s, destination visibility <=3 s, and scheduling/queue wait <=10 s. This totals <=25 s with 5 s margin; the relevant stages differ by direction. Assume webhook duplicates add no more than one extra delivery per event within any 30-second interval and normal webhook reordering stays within the delivery bound. These are contract requirements, not percentiles. Arbitrarily delayed delivery, unavailable dependencies, unbounded retries, or overload invalidate the bound even if both servers are running.

The 30-second target applies in healthy steady state. Catch-up has a separate target under the brief; do not promise fresh traffic within 30 s during catch-up until Phase 6 proves a scheduling and replay-safety policy. Resume the normal target only once replay is complete and queues fit the normal bounds. Preserve fresh work and prevent starvation in the later scheduler design.

Six-hour first estimates: portal backlog = `20 * 21,600 = 432,000` writes; Salesforce-origin history = `20 * 21,600 = 432,000` edits, before echoes, duplicates, and repairs. Quiet six-hour volumes would be 21,600 each. At the provisional allowance, outbound net drain is `90 - 20 - 10 = 60 requests/s`; `432,000 / 60 / 60 = 120 minutes`. This is only an outbound lower-detail estimate. Phase 6 must count history pagination, replay processing, repair amplification, outage-time echoes, storage, and synchronization barriers before adopting any end-to-end recovery time.

## Payload, identity, and provenance contract

Use changed-field patches provisionally. An omitted key means no edit; an explicit `null` means a deliberate clear only on a nullable field; a non-null value is a set (including an empty string only if the field schema permits it). A dedicated clear operation, if supplied upstream, must normalize to the same clear semantics. Required fields cannot be cleared. A supplied equal value with a distinct source edit identity remains a distinct edit in history; an unchanged field absent from a patch produces no field edit. Never infer a clear from omission.

A full-record webhook needs an authoritative changed-field mask or per-field versions and attributable change history. Comparing a delayed snapshot to our current row can mistake unchanged values for new edits and erase independent changes. Without this metadata, a full snapshot may support explicitly limited state reconciliation, but cannot satisfy complete edit history or safely arbitrate per-field conflicts. Reject/quarantine unsupported payload semantics rather than silently treating every field as changed.

Keep the sample's eventId, recordId, objectType, occurredAt, changedBy, and changedFields. Require tenant/source scope for IDs; an immutable source commit sequence/revision per record; replay partition and cursor; operation (create/update); schema version; and, for integration-origin writes, a stable mutation ID and original edit provenance. Source revision orders same-source commits; replay cursor tracks coverage, not cross-system conflict precedence. Require source-generated versions and audit identity to survive replay unchanged. Envelope authentication must protect these fields. Event identity deduplicates delivery; mutation identity correlates retries/echoes; neither is replaced by a value hash.

Portal actor comes from the authenticated server session's stable subject, with display email as descriptive metadata; the body’s editedBy is not authority. Assign commit time and a monotonic record revision on the portal server within the durable save boundary. Retain source occurredAt and local receivedAt separately. Assume synchronized source/server clocks within +/-1 second of UTC (up to 2 seconds relative skew); timestamps do not prove physical order across systems, especially within that uncertainty. Phase 2 must choose a deterministic business conflict policy, same-side serialization, and ties using named metadata, rather than claiming knowledge of the actual last human action.

Use a stable portal-generated UUID for new portal entities and an immutable Salesforce record ID for remote entities, mapped uniquely by tenant and object type. Do not automatically match by email, name, or phone. Cross-system duplicate business entities require explicit reconciliation. Map owners through a maintained stable principal-ID mapping; preserve the source owner identifier and actor even if the principal is inactive. Unknown owners/dependent customers must remain recoverable without substituting an unrelated owner or customer. Contact creates require a mapped customer first; customer reassignment is an explicit field edit. Assume parents/owners can normally be resolved within the latency envelope, otherwise expose a blocked item.

## Required upstream capabilities and feasibility gates

1. **Replay/audit:** replay every distinct committed edit, including intermediate values, actor identity, source time, stable event identity, changed fields, source revision, and provenance. Choose at least **72 hours** retention measured from source commit: 6 h outage + provisional 2 h recovery + 64 h operating margin. This is a retention assumption, not proof that catch-up fits 2 h. Alert before the remaining retention falls below estimated catch-up plus safety time; Phase 6 will refine it. Pagination must have stable ordering, resumable cursors, an explicit high-water mark/completion boundary, and defined partition gaps. Commit a contiguous checkpoint only with the corresponding durable history/application results; replay and webhooks must preserve identical identities. If interrupted beyond retention, detect the gap instead of reporting complete recovery.
2. **Safe create:** atomic upsert/unique external ID scoped to tenant and object, or durable upstream idempotency keys, retained for at least the full retry/recovery window (provisionally 72 h). A remote acceptance followed by a lost response must be resolvable to the same record. If uniqueness/key retention is unavailable, ambiguous creates cannot be retried with a no-duplicates guarantee; require reconciliation or an upstream change.
3. **Safe update:** field patches plus atomic conditional writes against a source record revision, and a read exposing that revision and field state. A failed precondition must have no effect. Read/merge/retry and conflict repairs consume outbound budget. Provenance must let replay distinguish a propagated edit from a new human edit without suppressing all changes made by an integration account. Without conditional writes, correction may eventually converge only under quiescence and weaker race assumptions; do not promise protection against unseen concurrent remote edits.

These gates require client confirmation or official-documentation verification before production commitment. Salesforce stays as it is under the brief: if its existing interfaces cannot supply the gates, identify the required integration/contract or requirement change rather than assume permission to modify it. A modified-record snapshot query restores some current state; it cannot reconstruct multiple overwritten edits or their actors. Missing replay therefore leaves the history requirement unmet. Authentic history prior to integration start likewise cannot be invented.

## Phase 2 refinements

`conflict-rules.md` selects source-revision candidates per field, followed by timestamp comparison and a PORTAL preference on exact ties. This resolves Q05/Q07 provisionally without claiming physical last-human-edit order. Both source candidates must be retained, particularly when source clocks roll back. Portal stale patch saves are accepted and audited; responses return the resolved current value, which need not be the saved value if a remote candidate wins.

Q09 is strengthened: before a conditional remote write at revision R, incorporate complete original edit history through R; a snapshot read with R alone is insufficient. Q10 requires per-field provenance for mixed events and separately attributable side effects. These refinements prevent unseen remote changes and integration echoes from being treated incorrectly. Additional coverage reads/history pages may exceed the one-write baseline and 10 requests/s overhead; Phase 6 must account for them. No coalescing is introduced: obsolete field applications may be explicitly superseded while their edit facts and individual work records remain retained. Q21 makes the history location assumption explicit.

## Phase 5 refinements

`outbound-flow.md` makes Q14's shared cap concrete: one fenced egress dispatcher paces actual client request initiations at C ≤100/s (90/s operating), without burst credit, hidden HTTP retries, or private worker budgets. Failover requires confirmed termination/fencing of the old sender and a one-second restart pause; database lease expiry alone is insufficient. Q17 therefore includes durable egress ownership/supervision. If termination cannot be established, dispatch pauses while portal saves remain durable. An upstream arrival-based cap would need a separate contract because network delays can bunch arrivals. Persist account cooldown and reduced effective rate alongside budget state.

Q08 requires create-or-resolve retries to return the existing identity without reinitializing its fields; a generic destructive upsert is insufficient. Q09 also requires safe resolution/fencing of outstanding ambiguous updates before a rebuilt mutation can bypass them. Read-only absence of an application while a request may still execute is not proof of no effect. These are explicit refinements of the existing safe-create/conditional-write gates, not verified capabilities. Phase 5 timing/backoff and fenced failover are design choices; Phase 6 still must calculate history/read/request amplification and catch-up service before accepting any recovery estimate.

## Phase 1 validation

Phase 3 clarification: `data-model.md` defines portal authoritative time as database server time sampled at the serialized decision boundary inside the save transaction, committed atomically with its revision/edit. This is not the later PostgreSQL WAL commit timestamp. `architecture.md` and the data model expose unresolved owner/customer references as durable history/candidates plus blocked lifecycle; acceptance does not imply synchronization or the 30-second bound for these dependency exceptions. No upstream capability assumption is verified or relaxed by these clarifications.
